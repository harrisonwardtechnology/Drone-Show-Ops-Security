# For Venues

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. You are hiring a thousand flying computers to perform over your crowd. Here is what to ask before you sign.

**Purpose.** The buyer's-side page: due diligence questions for venues, municipalities, and brands hiring a drone show operator, in the [right-sized questionnaire spirit](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/TPRM-RightSized), a dozen questions that change the decision, not three hundred that decorate a file.

## The Dozen Questions

1. What authorization does this show fly under, and can we see it? In the US that is FAA paperwork with your event's name on it, and an operator who bristles at the question has answered it.
2. Who is the pilot in command, what is their experience at this fleet size, and who is the backup?
3. What are your written abort criteria, and who on the crew can call an abort? The right answer includes junior people, per [show day](show-day.md).
4. What happens to every aircraft if it loses its command link or its position confidence? The answer should be specific, configured, and boring.
5. What is your exclusion zone over and around our crowd, and how is it enforced in the choreography, not just hoped?
6. Have you surveyed this site's RF and GPS environment, or will show night be the first look?
7. What is your loss history, aircraft down at shows, and what changed after? Zero-loss claims from a high-volume operator are their own answer.
8. How is the [ground station](the-ground-station.md) secured, and who has access to it during our event?
9. Where does your [fleet firmware](the-fleet.md) come from, and when was this fleet last updated relative to our show?
10. What insurance covers this operation, at what limits, and does it actually cover autonomous swarm operations, ask the broker, not the deck.
11. Who speaks publicly if something goes wrong at our event, and can we agree the answer now?
12. What do you need from us, power, perimeter, radio discipline, crowd lines, and what have venues gotten wrong before?

## Reading the Answers

You are listening for the [same qualities as any vendor review](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/TPRM-RightSized): specificity, written artifacts behind the words, and comfort with the question. Great operators answer question 12 at length, because they have been burned by venues before, and they treat questions 3 and 4 as chances to show off. The worrying answers are smooth generalities, "fully redundant, totally safe, never had an issue," which in a young industry means either extraordinary luck or short memory.

## The Contract Lines

Beyond the standard event terms: the authorization paperwork as an exhibit, abort authority and criteria acknowledged, the exclusion zones drawn, insurance certificates naming the venue, incident notification and joint-statement terms agreed in advance per [show day's](show-day.md) one-voice rule, and data terms if the show collects anything about your crowd. None of it is exotic; all of it is easier before signature, which is [the oldest rule in this library](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/TPRM-RightSized).
