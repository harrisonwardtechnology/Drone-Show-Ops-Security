# The Fleet

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. A thousand identical computers, one update away from a thousand identical problems.

**Purpose.** Firmware discipline, physical custody, and the inventory habits that keep a fleet a fleet instead of a swarm of question marks.

## One Update, Every Airframe

Fleet firmware is your own supply chain risk concentrated: a single update reaches every aircraft, which makes the update process itself the control. The discipline: firmware only from the manufacturer's verified channel, staged rollouts, a test batch flies it before the fleet wears it, a known-good version archived and restorable, and a written rule about timing, no new firmware inside the final days before a show, because the update that improves everything occasionally improves nothing, and discovering that over a crowd is not a rollback plan. Log what version every airframe runs; mixed-version fleets behave in mixed ways, and the [show file](the-ground-station.md) rehearsed against one behavior.

## The Inventory Is the Fleet

Every airframe has an identity: serial, firmware version, battery history, maintenance state, and current disposition, in a system, not a memory. The inventory is what makes every other control real, staged rollouts need to know what is staged, incident reconstruction needs to know which aircraft did what, and the [venue's insurance questions](for-venues.md) get answered from it in minutes. It is the [asset-inventory first principle](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/Security-Program-Starter) with propellers, and like every inventory, it is either maintained as things change or rebuilt archaeologically at the worst time.

## Batteries and Custody

Batteries are the fleet's physical risk concentrated, fire on the ground, failure in the air, so charging, storage, transport, and retirement follow the manufacturer's rules with logs to prove it. Custody covers the rest: aircraft and support gear travel counted, stored locked, and staged behind the crew perimeter, because a fleet spread across a public field during setup is an interference surface, tampering, theft, or innocent curiosity, and the counter is the oldest control in the book, someone responsible, watching, counting them out and counting them back.

## Spares and Degradation

The show is designed to survive losses: spare airframes prepped, and the choreography's behavior with holes in it understood in advance, which drones matter most, what the minimum viable show is, at what loss count the [abort criteria](show-day.md) fire. Degradation planning is where fleet security meets showmanship: an audience never notices twelve missing drones handled gracefully, and always notices one handled badly.

## Between Shows

The quiet season is when fleets rot: firmware drifts, batteries age unlogged, and the inventory's truth decays. A light maintenance rhythm, periodic power-on checks, battery cycling per spec, inventory reconciliation, keeps the fleet a known quantity, so that show prep starts from truth instead of triage. The [tabletop instinct](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/tabletop-library) applies here too: once a year, walk the failure of your own fleet processes on paper, lost custody, bad update, battery event, before a show night walks it for you.
